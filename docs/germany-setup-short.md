# Short version — to send to Vedant

---

Hey — I looked into this properly, because it's a fair thing to worry about and I didn't want to just
guess. Short version: **you're safe, and it's much smaller than you think.** But the worry is pointed
at the wrong thing.

**Putting prices on a website doesn't create any obligation.** German law is clear that you count as a
business the moment you sustainably do something to earn money — the law says this applies *even if
you're not making a profit*. Same for the duty to tell the tax office. It follows from teaching a
paid class, not from what your website says. Taking the prices off changes nothing you owe. It just
means fewer people book.

**The good news: dance teaching is a *freier Beruf*.** The federal tax court confirmed this
specifically for dance teachers. That means **no trade tax, no IHK membership and no IHK fees.** You
fill in one free form online (ELSTER), within a month of your first paid class. That's it to start.

**What you'd actually pay.** If you took €800/month in fees with €300/month of costs, split between
you:

- VAT: **€0** — you're way under the €25,000 small-business threshold, and you don't even file VAT returns
- Trade tax: **€0**
- Income tax if you have no other income: **€0** — you'd each be under the €12,348 tax-free allowance
- Income tax if you both have jobs: roughly **€1,000 each for the year**

So realistically: one free form now, one tax return a year, and somewhere between nothing and about
a thousand euros each. That's the whole burden.

**Three things you probably don't know:**

1. **Self-employed teachers have to pay into the state pension** — teachers are named specifically in
   the law. You're exempt while you expect to earn under **€603/month each**, which at the numbers
   above you are. But it kicks in as you grow. This is the one that actually costs money, so ask
   about it.
2. **Teaching together as a pair can accidentally make you a legal partnership (GbR)**, which means an
   extra annual filing — and if you ever sell drinks at a social, it can drag *all* your teaching
   income into trade tax. Easily avoided by each invoicing your own students. Worth sorting before
   you start, not after.
3. **You need an Impressum with a real street address** — and if you work from home, that's your home
   address, published. That applies to a website with no prices too, and it applies to a business
   Instagram profile. Might be the one thing here you actually don't like.

**On the Instagram + Google Form idea — honestly, it's worse.** The Impressum rule follows you to
Instagram, the online-selling rules still apply to a form, and a free Google account doesn't give you
the data-protection agreement German law needs, which no privacy policy can fix. It keeps every
obligation you were trying to avoid, adds a new one, and scatters your income records across
something you can't cleanly hand the tax office.

**And the bit worth saying plainly:** registering a bit late but declaring honestly is
administrative — you'd pay the tax and a little interest, and at your size realistically no fine.
*Not* declaring is a criminal offence. Staying hidden turns a non-problem into a real one. The tax
office does crawl the web, but it's looking for people who never registered — a registered business
with public prices is completely unremarkable.

**What I'd do:** spend an hour on the ELSTER form, publish the site as it is, and book one paid
session with a Steuerberater — an hour of their time will settle the pension question, the
partnership question, and confirm the small-business status. Munich also has a free founders' advice
office (Münchner Gründungsbüro) if you want a sanity check first.

If you still want to start without prices for a few weeks while you settle on numbers, that's
completely fine and takes me ten minutes to change — just don't do it thinking it protects you,
because it doesn't.
