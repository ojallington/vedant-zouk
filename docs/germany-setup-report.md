# Starting a small dance-teaching business in Munich

**Research for Miriam & Pavan · 4 September 2026**

This exists because of a specific worry: that a professional-looking website with prices on it will
attract the attention of the German tax authorities, and that it might be safer to hide the prices
and take bookings quietly through Instagram until there's real money coming in.

The short answer is that the worry is pointed at the wrong thing — and that the real obligations are
smaller and cheaper than they probably imagine. But there are three things in here they genuinely
don't know about, and one of them costs money.

> Research, not legal or tax advice. Sources are linked throughout. Section 9 lists the specific
> questions worth putting to a Steuerberater — a one-off Erstberatung is inexpensive and would settle
> all of them.

---

## 1. The short answer

**Publishing prices creates no obligation that hiding them avoids.**

Every duty they're worried about attaches to the *activity*, not to the website:

- You are an *Unternehmer* for VAT purposes if you carry on a sustained activity to earn income —
  [§ 2 Abs. 1 UStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__2.html) says this applies
  **even without profit intent**.
- The duty to notify the tax office is triggered by *taking up* the activity —
  [§ 138 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__138.html), within one month.

Teaching a paid weekly class meets both, whether the price is on a website, in an Instagram DM, or
spoken out loud in the studio. Removing prices from the site changes nothing they owe. It only costs
them students.

**The honest other half.** The tax administration does search the web — its XPIDER crawler scans in
the order of 100,000 pages a day looking for signs of undeclared business activity. But it is hunting
people who *haven't* registered. For a registered business, a public price list is ordinary
advertising. A business that is visibly running while conspicuously hiding what it charges is closer
to the shape that draws attention, not further from it.

**And the risk they're actually taking.** Registering late but declaring honestly is an
administrative matter — back-tax plus about 0.15%/month interest, and at their scale realistically no
fine. Not declaring income at all is
[§ 370 AO Steuerhinterziehung](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__370.html), a criminal
offence. The plan to "stay hidden" turns a nothing-problem into a serious one.

## 2. The good news: this is a *freier Beruf*

Teaching dance is *unterrichtende Tätigkeit* under
[§ 18 Abs. 1 Nr. 1 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__18.html). The Bundesfinanzhof has
confirmed this specifically for dance teachers (BFH 21.04.1994, IV R 99/93; BFH 18.05.1995,
IV R 31/94). For a sole trader, even a genuinely commercial sideline — selling drinks, say — doesn't
contaminate the teaching income; the two are assessed separately.

What that means in practice:

- **No Gewerbesteuer** (trade tax)
- **No IHK membership** and no IHK fees — [confirmed](https://www.ihk-muenchen.de/ueber-uns/beitrag/)
- **No Gewerbeanmeldung** from the tax side
- One free online form to start, and one annual tax return

**One genuine ambiguity.** Federal trade-law guidance
([BMWK](https://www.existenzgruendungsportal.de/Redaktion/DE/BMWK-Infopool/Antworten/Gruendungsplanung/Freie-Berufe/unterrichtende-Taetigkeit/Tanzkurse-anbieten-Anmeldung))
says dance tuition *is* a trade under the Gewerbeordnung and needs a Gewerbeanmeldung, while the tax
authorities treat it as freiberuflich. Both can be true at once — different offices, different tests.
In practice many dance teachers register a Gewerbe *and* are taxed as Freiberufler. It is a €50–60
question for the Munich KVR, not a blocker, and even if they are gewerblich the €24,500 trade-tax
allowance means they'd pay €0 anyway.

## 3. What they actually have to do

| # | Step | When | Cost | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fragebogen zur steuerlichen Erfassung** | Within **1 month** of the first paid class | **Free** | [ELSTER](https://www.elster.de) online |
| 2 | Steuernummer arrives | A few weeks later | — | By post |
| 3 | *If* the KVR says they need it: **Gewerbeanmeldung** | At the start of the activity | **€50–60** | KVR, Implerstraße 11, online via BayernID |
| 4 | Keep a spreadsheet of income and receipts | Ongoing | Free | — |
| 5 | **Einkommensteuererklärung** + Anlage S + Anlage EÜR | By **31 July** of the following year | Free via ELSTER | [ELSTER](https://www.elster.de) |

That's the whole thing. One form to start, one return a year.

**Nothing has to happen before the first class.** The one-month window runs from when they start, so
this doesn't delay anything.

**Free help in Munich:** the Münchner Gründungsbüro (city-run, free, covers freiberuflich too), MEB,
and the IHK's free initial consultation.

## 4. What they'd actually pay

**Worked example: €800/month in class fees, €300/month of costs.**

Turnover €9,600/yr, costs €3,600, profit €6,000 — split €3,000 each.

| | |
|---|---|
| VAT | **€0** — under the €25,000 Kleinunternehmer threshold. No VAT returns at all. |
| Trade tax | **€0** — freiberuflich; and under €24,500 regardless |
| Income tax, if no other income | **€0** — €3,000 each is under the €12,348 tax-free allowance |
| Income tax, if both employed on ~€50k | roughly **€1,000–1,150 each** — the profit stacks on salary at ~35% |

So the worst case at that scale is about €2,100 of income tax on €9,600 of turnover, and the best
case is nothing at all. Everything else is zero.

**Kleinunternehmerregelung ([§ 19 UStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__19.html)),
reformed 1 Jan 2025:** exempt if turnover was under **€25,000** last year and stays under **€100,000**
this year. In the first year of trading only the €25,000 ceiling applies, and it is **not** pro-rated.
No VAT charged, no VAT returns. Invoices must carry a note such as *"steuerfreier Kleinunternehmer"*
and must **never** show a VAT amount — not even 0% — or it becomes payable.

**Deductible:** studio rent, music and licences, travel to the venue, liability insurance, the
website and hosting, advertising, training and workshops, sound equipment, teaching costumes.

## 5. Three things they don't know about

### 5.1 Teachers are compulsorily pension-insured — this one costs money

Self-employed teachers with no insured employee fall under
[§ 2 S. 1 Nr. 1 SGB VI](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_6/__2.html), which names teachers
explicitly. This is the one real cost nobody expects.

The way out is the *geringfügig* exception: expected profit at or under the Geringfügigkeitsgrenze,
**€603/month in 2026**. In the worked example above they're at roughly €250/month each, comfortably
under. But it scales with success, and the usual three-year founder exemption
(§ 6 Abs. 1a SGB VI) does **not** cover teachers.

**Worth asking the Steuerberater about explicitly.** It's the single most likely thing to be
discovered late and awkwardly.

### 5.2 Teaching together may create a GbR by accident

If they jointly run the courses and split the proceeds, a GbR comes into existence automatically
under [§ 705 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__705.html) whether they intend it or not.
That means an extra annual filing (a *gesonderte und einheitliche Feststellungserklärung*).

Worse, [§ 15 Abs. 3 Nr. 1 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__15.html) means any
commercial activity by a partnership taints **all** its income — sell drinks at one social and the
entire teaching income becomes gewerblich. The tolerance is 3% of net turnover and at most €24,500.

**The simple fix:** two separate sole traders, each invoicing their own students for their own share.
No GbR, no filing, no contamination risk. Whether that's realistic given they teach as a pair is
exactly what to ask a Steuerberater.

Registering an *eGbR* in the Gesellschaftsregister is **not** mandatory for a teaching partnership.

### 5.3 The Impressum needs a real, physical address

[§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html) (which replaced § 5 TMG in May 2024)
requires a *ladungsfähige Anschrift* — a street address where legal post can be served. A P.O. box
isn't enough. **If they work from home, that home address has to be published.**

This applies to a brochure site with no prices, and it applies to a **commercial Instagram profile**
too — reachable within two clicks, labelled "Impressum" or "Kontakt" ("Info" doesn't count).

Relief: since the 2020 UWG reform, competitors can no longer claim their costs for Abmahnungen over
these information duties ([§ 13 Abs. 4 Nr. 1 UWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__13.html)).
Registered trade associations still can.

## 6. The options, compared

### Option A — Register properly, keep the site as built *(recommended)*

Fragebogen on ELSTER, publish prices, take bookings.

- **Time:** about an hour of forms, once
- **Cost:** €0, or €50–60 if the KVR wants a Gewerbeanmeldung
- **Tax:** €0 until profit passes €12,348 each; no VAT under €25,000
- **Risk:** none — this is simply what compliance looks like
- **Cost of it:** they have to publish a home address in the Impressum

### Option B — Soft launch: site with no prices, enquiries only

Course information, no prices, no booking; people email or DM to ask.

- Still needs an Impressum, still needs registering once they earn
- Legally **identical** to Option A in everything that matters
- Loses the thing the site is for: people deciding and booking without a conversation
- Reasonable only as a *temporary* step while they settle on pricing — not as protection

### Option C — Instagram profile + Google Form

- Avoids **nothing**: the Impressum duty follows them to Instagram, and distance-selling rules apply
  to a form booking just as much
- **Adds** a GDPR problem — a free Google account gives no AVV (data processing agreement), which no
  privacy policy can fix; that needs paid Workspace
- Scatters revenue records across a platform they can't cleanly export for the Finanzamt
- Strictly worse on every axis

**A quiet start is fine. A hidden one isn't.** If they want to begin gently, Option B for a few weeks
is harmless. But it should be a choice about readiness, not a shield — because it isn't one.

## 7. Website legal checklist

Things the site needs before it takes a real booking:

- [ ] **Impressum** — names, street address, email ([§ 5 DDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ddg/__5.html))
- [ ] **Datenschutzerklärung** — Art. 13 GDPR; booking data is processed under Art. 6(1)(b),
      contract performance. Name Stripe as a processor (it has an auto-incorporated DPA and is
      certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework)
- [ ] **Payment button wording** —
      [§ 312j Abs. 3 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312j.html) requires
      *"zahlungspflichtig bestellen"* or an unambiguous equivalent. **If the button is wrong there is
      no contract and no claim to payment** (§ 312j Abs. 4). *This was wrong in the build and has
      been fixed.*
- [ ] **Prices as gross totals**, with a Kleinunternehmer note and no VAT figure shown
      ([PAngV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/pangv_2022/BJNR492110021.html))
- [ ] **A line about withdrawal rights** — classes on a fixed date are excluded from the 14-day right
      by [§ 312g Abs. 2 Nr. 9 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__312g.html) (leisure
      services tied to a date). Say so plainly; the BGH has held that failing to mention it doesn't
      create the right, but it avoids arguments
- [ ] **AGB** — not legally required, but this is where cancellation, no-show and class-cancelled
      rules live. Without them the defaults run against the teacher
- [x] **Cookie banner** — **not needed**. The site has no analytics or tracking, and
      [§ 25 Abs. 2 TDDDG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/tddg/__25.html) exempts strictly
      necessary storage

## 8. If they still want prices hidden

Technically trivial — every price lives in one file (`shared/site-config.js`), so a prices-off build
is a few minutes' work and reversible at any time. Say the word.

It just shouldn't be done for the reason currently on the table, because it doesn't buy what they
think it buys.

## 9. Questions for a Steuerberater

Worth one paid Erstberatung. These are the questions:

1. Do we count as freiberuflich, and does the Munich KVR still expect a Gewerbeanmeldung?
2. Teaching as a couple — are we accidentally a GbR, and should we invoice separately instead?
3. Does § 2 S. 1 Nr. 1 SGB VI pension insurance apply to us, and are we under the €603/month
   exemption?
4. Kleinunternehmer — confirm we're in it and what our invoices must say.
5. We both have jobs. What does the side income do to our tax and our health insurance?
6. We rent a room from an existing Tanzstudio. Any Scheinselbständigkeit risk?

On (6): as long as they set their own prices, enrol their own students and keep their own schedule,
they're tenants and nothing shifts to the studio. The risk is the reverse arrangement — if the studio
recruits students, sets times and does the billing, the *Herrenberg* line of Bundessozialgericht
cases makes that Scheinselbständigkeit. A written room-rental contract settles it.

Statutes are linked inline. The main secondary sources: BMF letter of 18.03.2025 on the
Kleinunternehmer reform; FinMin Sachsen Erlass 16.02.2026 on VAT for dance schools (recreational

Two points where the sourcing is weaker, flagged honestly:

- **DAC7 / Plattformen-Steuertransparenzgesetz does not cover Stripe or PayPal**, which are payment
  processors rather than platforms, nor a business's own website. Commentary is near-unanimous but
  rests on advisory sources rather than a BMF ruling.
- Whether a rented dance studio counts as an *"Institut oder dergleichen"* for the
  [§ 12 PAngV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/pangv_2022/BJNR492110021.html) price-list exception
  could not be resolved. It points toward more price transparency, not less, so it doesn't change the
  advice.
