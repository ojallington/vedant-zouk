Read this bit first — it takes a minute.

Rather than guess at one design and get it wrong, I built five. They are not sketches: every one is a finished, working website. Same words, same prices, same booking — five different looks. You are choosing a feel, not a feature list.

In each one you can go the whole way through booking a class, choose card or cash, and see the confirmation. Nothing takes real money. Card payment is built and deliberately switched off until you say go.

Everything you see is easy to change afterwards — prices, times, words, photos. What is hard to change later is the overall look, so that is the one thing worth your attention today.

What I had to guess

  • The prices are invented. €12 trial, €18 a single class, €30 for both levels in one night, €65 for a four-week course, €95 for both levels across four weeks. I put them just under Zoukseed on Wednesdays and made them far simpler to compare — their pricing page hides five overlapping options behind a dropdown, which is an easy thing to beat. These are a starting point to react to, not a recommendation.
  • Levels 1 and 3, Thursdays at Tanzstudio Rebecca, White Room, 20:00 and 21:00. Taken from the schedule graphic — say if anything has moved.
  • The bit about you and Miriam is placeholder text that I wrote. Replace it with your own words.
  • Student logins work per-device for now. Someone's bookings are remembered on their own phone. Accounts that follow a person between devices need a small piece of backend — straightforward to add later, and it will not change whichever design you pick.

What I need back from you

You do not have to answer all of it. Numbers one and two unblock everything else — the rest can follow.

  1. Which one? Just the number. If you want the look of one and the pricing layout of another, say that — mixing is fine and normal.
  2. The five prices. Correct them, or tell me the structure is wrong. Related: is this a fixed four-week course people commit to, or can they drop in any Thursday? It changes what the whole page leads with.
  3. An email address for bookings to arrive at.
  4. Two or three sentences about you and Miriam — how you started, what you love about Zouk. Voice note is fine, I will write it up.
  5. English, German, or both? Munich dancers will expect German; the poster is English.
  6. Do you want card payments on? If yes I need a Stripe account connected. Cash at the door keeps working either way.
  7. Booking as a couple. Right now people can name a partner and say whether they lead or follow. Useful, or noise?
  8. Do you own a domain? If not I can suggest a few.

How to give feedback so it actually lands

  • Screenshot and scribble on it. One picture with an arrow beats a paragraph. Your phone will do it.
  • Tell me the feeling, not the fix. "This feels too cold for a dance class" is far more useful than "make the buttons blue" — the second one closes off better answers.
  • Say what you liked too. If I only hear the problems, I will change something that was already working.
  • Show it to two or three dancers before you reply, ideally someone who has never done Zouk. Ask them one question: could you book a class? Watch where they hesitate. That is worth more than any opinion either of us has.
  • Do not worry about being harsh. Throwing one of these away costs nothing — that is exactly why there are five.
